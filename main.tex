\heading{Project Summary}
\begin{center}
\textbf{Project: Cloud-Accelerated NeuroFrame: Geometry-Aware Foundation Models for Extracellular Electrophysiology}\\
\textbf{PI: Erdem Varol, New York University}
\end{center}

Large-scale electrophysiology is difficult to compare across animals, laboratories, and probe designs because recordings are usually referenced by targeting procedures rather than validated biological coordinates. The same nominal brain region can contain different tissue, cells, and molecular environments. This hidden sampling variation reduces reproducibility and can make an apparent task effect look biological or behavioral.

We will introduce NeuroFrame as a new cloud-accelerated research program for biologically grounded registration of extracellular recordings. The immediate goal is to determine how accurately self-supervised foundation models can infer atlas position from extracellular recordings and probe geometry on held-out animals and recording systems. We will scale geometry-aware local-field-potential encoders beyond the current 95-million-parameter, 1-million-sample limit; train converged models at larger capacity and corpus sizes; and extend the representation to native 30-kHz action-potential recordings (AP2vec). Each model will return a predictive distribution, not only a point estimate, and will be evaluated with animal-level splits, correspondence-breaking controls, calibration, uncertainty, and abstention when evidence does not support fine localization.

Google Cloud is essential because the longest multi-GPU jobs exceed NYU Torch's single-allocation wall-time/GPU policy, while the AP corpus requires substantially more memory, local staging, and I/O than the current pipeline. We will use public Allen Brain Observatory, IBL Brain-Wide Map, and DANDI data only. The award will produce converged scaling measurements, released checkpoints, reproducible evaluation code, and an interactive atlas demonstration, strengthening future NSF, NIH BRAIN, NeuroNex, CRCNS, and related proposals.

\heading{Research Project}
\subheading{1. Importance and motivation}

Large-scale neural recordings now span many animals, laboratories, behavioral paradigms, and probe designs, but the biological reference for a recording is often an informal targeting coordinate, so two experiments labeled as the same region may sample different atlas positions, local populations, or molecular environments. Pooling such recordings can confound task effects with sampling differences; analyzing every animal independently sacrifices statistical power. Histology remains essential for many studies, but it is post hoc, static, and does not provide a real-time estimate of electrical-source position or tissue motion.

NeuroFrame's central hypothesis is that the spatial structure of extracellular activity relative to known probe geometry contains enough information to estimate biological position at a resolution supported by held-out data. This project will establish the first two technical pillars of that new program: atlas registration from LFP and probe geometry, and a native action-potential extension that tests whether the same idea can access finer-scale spatial and cellular information.

Preliminary work establishes feasibility (Figure~\ref{fig:prelim}). On held-out IBL animals, geometry-aware self-supervision plus a probe-sequence decoder reaches approximately 1.29 mm raw three-dimensional localization error, and 1.20 mm after rigid probe-geometry refinement. Increasing self-supervised data from 25k to 1M samples improves frozen-decoder performance monotonically at every rung, while prior experiments have used essentially the same wav2vec2-base backbone (approximately 95M parameters). Data have therefore been scaled by a factor of forty while model capacity has been held fixed, and the per-decade gain is narrowing. The key unanswered question is whether that narrowing reflects insufficient model capacity and incomplete pretraining rather than a limit of the signal. A second unresolved question is whether native 30-kHz action-potential modeling can recover information discarded by the current single-channel, downsampled AP transfer pipeline.

\subheading{2. Goals and aims}
\textbf{Aim 1 -- Converged capacity and data scaling.} Train geometry-aware LFP encoders at approximately 95M, 317M, and 1B parameters across validated corpus sizes from the existing 1M scale toward 10M and 32M chunks. Complete intended training schedules instead of relying on early snapshots forced by local wall-time limits. Evaluate every headline cell with frozen linear, nonlinear, and sequence readouts on held-out animals and probes.

\textbf{Aim 2 -- Native AP2vec feasibility.} Train a native 30-kHz action-potential encoder with a multi-channel spatial frontend. The current transfer experiment resamples AP to the LFP rate and uses one channel, producing a negative held-out result; it therefore cannot test the spatial waveform information that motivates AP2vec. We will establish whether native sampling and neighboring-contact context improve source/localization representations, while reporting negative results if they do not.

\textbf{Aim 3 -- Molecular and cell-type context with calibrated registration.} Propagate atlas-position uncertainty into atlas-derived gene-expression and molecular annotations, and test whether the same learned electrical representations predict independently measured cell types and molecular context. For every model, report raw coordinate error, axis-specific error, atlas-region accuracy, predictive uncertainty, calibration, and answer/abstention rate using complete animal/session/probe holdouts and correspondence-breaking controls.

\textbf{Aim 4 -- Behavior decoding from the same foundation model.} Without training a separate behavior-specific encoder, freeze the LFP/AP foundation representation and use lightweight readouts to decode stimulus side, choice, and wheel movement in locked IBL windows, with an optional ITI-prior plus signed-contrast policy analysis. Evaluate subject- and probe-disjoint transfer and compare neural-only predictions with behavior-only baselines. This tests whether the same representation captures behaviorally relevant information while separating task signal from anatomical and molecular sampling differences.

\subheading{3. Methodology}
We will use public Allen Brain Observatory, IBL Brain-Wide Map, and DANDI resources; human intracranial recordings are outside this award's scope and remain on NYU infrastructure under existing agreements. Dataset manifests will freeze animal/session/probe membership, preprocessing, probe geometry, labels, exclusions, and train/calibration/test splits before final evaluation. The LFP model retains the validated masked-prediction, temporal-contrastive, and codebook-diversity objectives, augmented by geometry-aware distance supervision from relative probe layout; absolute channel identifiers and atlas coordinates are never supplied as encoder shortcuts. The AP model uses native 30-kHz windows, neighboring-contact context, and the same correspondence-aware design.

Every stage is checkpointed, recording software, hardware, seed, manifest, effective sample size, and cloud cost. Evaluation proceeds at three levels: held-out animals within represented recording systems; zero-shot laboratory, probe-family, or system transfer; and prespecified few-animal recalibration if zero-shot transfer fails. Independent animals, sessions, probes, or preparations--not windows or contacts--determine confidence intervals. Formal conformal coverage claims are made only for exchangeable calibration/test cohorts; under declared distribution shift we report empirical coverage and abstain when overlap diagnostics do not support a calibrated answer.

\heading{Research Project (continued)}
\subheading{4. GCP-enabled work and timeline}
\textbf{August 2026 -- portability and efficiency gate.} Containerize the validated training/evaluation pipeline; stage sharded public corpora on Cloud Storage and node-local SSD; benchmark mixed precision with fp32 master weights, FlashAttention, gradient accumulation, distributed loading, and FSDP; reproduce the current LFP baselines on GCP before launching the scaling grid.

\textbf{September -- LFP capacity/data grid.} Run the 95M/317M/1B and 10M/32M corpus cells that pass the efficiency gate, with checkpointed multi-GPU jobs long enough to avoid local wall-time truncation. The existing 1M column provides the baseline; GCP funds the larger corpus columns and capacity comparison.

\textbf{October -- AP2vec and replication.} Train native AP models on the H200-capable configuration, run the highest-priority LFP/AP seed replications, and perform held-out-system evaluations. Stop or narrow any arm that fails its prespecified memory, throughput, or correspondence-control gate.

\textbf{November 1--25 -- evaluation and release.} Freeze checkpoints, run calibration and uncertainty analyses, generate scaling and failure-mode figures, publish reproducible manifests/code, and deploy a small interactive atlas demonstration. November 26--30 is reserved for final artifact egress and account closure; no new uncheckpointed experiments will be started.

\subheading{6. Expected outcomes and significance}
The minimum outcome is a rigorously measured, converged capacity/data scaling curve and a reproducible answer about whether native AP modeling adds information beyond LFP. Positive results would establish a new route to biologically grounded registration across recording systems; negative results would still identify a tested resolution or architecture limit and prevent the field from interpreting truncated or downsampled runs as evidence of failure. Released checkpoints, animal-level splits, calibration tools, and evaluation code will establish a strong foundation for future NeuroFrame proposals and external funding.

\subheading{Why GCP materially improves on NYU resources}
NYU Torch supports ordinary short and single-GPU jobs, but the target workloads combine multi-GPU training, multi-day uninterrupted execution, high host memory, large local staging, and I/O. The measured 95M/1M pretraining run required approximately 747 GPU-hours and had to be chained across wall-time-limited jobs. Native AP additionally requires substantially more memory and staging than the LFP pipeline. GCP supplies the simultaneous multi-GPU and multi-day window, H200 memory, bundled node-local SSD, and Cloud Batch orchestration needed for auditable, checkpointed workflows.

\subheading{Governance and cost control}
We will use containerized jobs, versioned manifests, least-privilege project access, and logs recording configuration, seed, split, hardware, cost, and artifact checksum. Cloud Storage will contain public Allen, IBL, and DANDI data plus derived research artifacts; human intracranial data are excluded. Jobs that fail memory, throughput, or correspondence-control criteria will be stopped and their allocation redirected to prespecified evaluation or replication tasks. Vertex AI managed-training fees, Vizier trials, committed-use contracts, and TPU work are excluded from the requested budget.

\heading{Future Funding Plans}
This award will establish NeuroFrame as a measured, reproducible research program. It will provide three assets that reviewers routinely require but that cannot be produced reliably under NYU Torch's current allocation shape: (1) converged capacity/data scaling curves rather than wall-time-truncated snapshots; (2) a technically honest native-AP feasibility result, including a well-controlled negative result if AP2vec does not improve localization; and (3) released checkpoints, animal-level manifests, calibration analyses, and correspondence-breaking controls that make the claims independently testable.
